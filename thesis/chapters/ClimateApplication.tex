\chapter{Climate-Model Application} \label{ch:climate}

This chapter connects the mathematical and numerical results of the thesis to a climate-model workflow. Its purpose is not to shift the conceptual center of the thesis away from randomized numerical linear algebra, but to test whether the methods studied in the previous chapters remain meaningful once they are placed inside a realistic downstream task.

In this setting, the relevant objects are no longer only synthetic matrices with controlled properties, but data structures shaped by the statistical post-processing of climate simulations. That change matters because the geometry of the problem is now inherited from a concrete application: dependence between variables, spatial correlations, temporal structure, and the scale of the underlying datasets all influence what it means for a randomized compression or a reduced-precision computation to be acceptable.

The main role of the application is therefore evaluative. It asks whether randomized and mixed-precision methods remain useful in a realistic downstream setting, and if so, in what sense. Utility here should be interpreted broadly. A method may be valuable because it reduces runtime, because it lowers memory consumption, because it enables a larger problem to be treated within fixed hardware limits, or because it reveals a numerically stable regime that would not have been obvious from synthetic tests alone. Conversely, a negative result is also informative if it identifies a failure mode tied to conditioning, loss of structure, or sensitivity to lower precision.

From the thesis perspective, the application chapter is thus a test of transfer. The earlier chapters develop a framework in terms of embeddings, reduced operators, residuals, solution errors, and floating-point perturbations. The present chapter asks how much of that framework survives contact with a genuine computational workflow in climate-data post-processing. The answer need not be a dramatic speedup. It is equally useful if the application clarifies where the numerical assumptions behind the randomized or mixed-precision methods are realistic and where they begin to break down.
