\chapter{Notation \& Conventions} \label{ch:notation}
\markboth{Notation \& Conventions}{Notation \& Conventions}

Throughout the thesis, matrices are denoted by uppercase Latin letters such as \(A\), \(S\), and \(Q\), while vectors are denoted by lowercase letters such as \(x\) and \(b\). The dimensions of a least-squares problem are typically written as follows: \(A \in \R^{n \times d}\) with \(n \ge d\), and the goal is to solve an overdetermined system
\[
    \min_{x \in \R^d} \|Ax-b\|_2.
\]

The symbol \(S\) is reserved for a sketching matrix. A sketched least-squares problem therefore has the form
\[
    \min_{x \in \R^d} \|SAx-Sb\|_2.
\]

Unless stated otherwise, \(\|\cdot\|_2\) denotes the Euclidean norm for vectors and the corresponding operator norm for matrices, while \(\|\cdot\|_F\) denotes the Frobenius norm. The condition number of a matrix \(M\) in the Euclidean norm is written as
\[
    \kappa_2(M) = \|M\|_2 \|M^{-1}\|_2
\]
whenever \(M\) is nonsingular.

Floating-point quantities are discussed using the standard model of rounding to nearest. The unit roundoff of a format is denoted by \(u\). When mixed precision is used, different unit roundoffs may appear in different parts of the same algorithm; these are distinguished by subscripts such as \(u_\ell\) for lower precision and \(u_h\) for higher precision.

In numerical experiments, the terms \enquote{solution error} and \enquote{residual error} are used in the following sense:
\begin{itemize}
    \item solution error compares an approximation \(\hat x\) directly with a reference solution \(x_\star\), typically through \(\|\hat x - x_\star\|_2\) or a relative variant,
    \item residual error measures how well \(\hat x\) satisfies the original problem through quantities such as \(\|A\hat x - b\|_2\).
\end{itemize}

Gaussian sketches, structured sketches, and mixed-precision variants are introduced more carefully in later chapters, but the notation fixed here remains standard throughout the text.
