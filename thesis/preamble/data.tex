%% Abstract (recommended length around 80-200 words; this is not a copy of your thesis assignment!)
\newcommand*{\Abstract}{%
	This thesis studies randomized numerical linear algebra in mixed-precision
	arithmetic, with climate-model post-processing as a downstream application.
	The central mathematical objective is to understand how randomized
	approximation and floating-point perturbations interact when large linear
	algebra problems are compressed and solved approximately. Randomized
	least-squares methods provide the first model setting, where sketching,
	conditioning, and solver stability can be analyzed in a clean form. The
	second main setting is a regularized covariance-transport map for
	multivariate bias correction, where empirical covariance operators are
	approximated by randomized low-rank surrogates and the effect of precision,
	rank, and regularization can be studied at the operator level. The thesis
	combines theory, numerical experiments, and prototype software in R and
	Octave to identify regimes in which reduced precision remains acceptable
	because arithmetic error stays below randomized approximation error.
}
